1. What This Library Is For — and Isn't

Every entry below earns its place by validating a specific AviOS design decision or market claim: the MSG-3 logic in Layer 3 of the simulator, the failure-rate calibration in Layer 2, the cost-of-AOG story in the pitch, the market-size number on the first slide. Sources that are merely "about aviation" are excluded.

Honesty boundary, same as always: literature gives you design validity (your architecture matches how the industry actually works) and market validity (the spend and the pain are real and independently documented). It cannot give you predictive validity — that still comes only from the operator extract defined in the Real-Data Ask spec. "Certainty AviOS will revolutionize the market" is not available from any bookshelf; what is available is the evidence base that makes the thesis defensible in every meeting until the pilot data lands.

2. Tier 1 — The Spine (read these first)

2.1 Aviation Maintenance Management — Kinnison & Siddiqui BOOK

McGraw-Hill, 2nd ed. The standard end-to-end text on how an airline M&E organization actually runs: maintenance program structure, QA/QC, reliability programs, materiel, planning, and the org chart your buyers sit in. Kinnison spent 20 years at Boeing customer services. Validates: Layer 3 of the simulator (task cards, planning, reliability program feedback loop) and the entity model's fidelity to real M&E departments — this book is effectively a map of your user personas.

2.2 Aircraft Maintenance Programs — David Lapesa Barrera BOOK

Springer Series in Reliability Engineering, 2022. The first systematic EASA-vs-FAA comparison of maintenance program requirements, with full MSG-3 methodology and the accident history behind each rule. Written by the engineer who managed the A220 launch-operator AMP and the world's largest A380 fleet program. Validates: the MSG-3 engine and the Part-145/Part-M/Part 43 rules in Layer 4 — this is the single closest book to the simulator's Layers 3–4 and should be treated as their reference implementation.

2.3 Reliability-Centered Maintenance — Nowlan & Heap (1978) FREE PDF

The United Airlines / DoD report that founded the entire field: why hard-time overhaul fails for most failure modes, the six failure patterns, and the logic that became MSG-3 and SAE JA1011. Freely available (PDF mirror). Validates: AviOS's core premise. The famous finding — only ~11% of failure modes are age-related, so scheduled overhaul can't prevent the other ~89% — is the 48-year-old academic argument for condition-based prediction. Cite it; sophisticated buyers know it.

2.4 IATA Maintenance Cost data eXchange (MCX) Executive Reports FREE (PUBLIC VERSION)

Annual benchmarking from 50+ airlines (~a quarter of world fleet): maintenance cost per flight hour, per ATA segment, labor vs material split. Public versions: FY2024, FY2023; program home at iata.org/mcx. Validates: every cost number in the AviOS ROI model, and the simulator's cost calibration. When a buyer challenges your savings math, this is the neutral referee.

2.5 Oliver Wyman Global Fleet & MRO Market Forecast 2026–2036 FREE

The annually-refreshed market sizing the whole industry quotes: ~30,000 aircraft in service growing to ~41,000 by 2036 (3.2% CAGR); MRO spend past $136B in 2025; average fleet age up to 13.4 years on OEM production shortfalls. Pair with IATA's estimate that supply-chain bottlenecks cost airlines $11B+ in 2025, $3.1B of it extra maintenance (IATA press release) and ARSA's annual market assessment. Validates: the TAM slide and, more importantly, the timing argument — an aging, supply-constrained fleet is precisely the market where predicting failures and pre-positioning parts is worth the most.

3. Tier 2 — The Predictive Core

3.1 The IVHM Series — Ian K. Jennions (ed.), SAE International 4 BOOKS

From Cranfield's IVHM Centre: Perspectives on an Emerging Field, Business Case Theory and Practice, The Technology (R-429), and Implementation and Lessons Learned (R-438). The only book series covering exactly what AviOS is: health management integrated into maintenance operations, including why deployments fail. Validates: the whole product thesis. Business Case arms the CFO conversation; Implementation and Lessons Learned is a catalogue of the mistakes you get to skip.

3.2 Prognostics and Health Management of Engineering Systems — Kim, An & Choi BOOK

Springer, 2017. The standard graduate introduction to RUL estimation: physics-based vs data-driven methods, uncertainty quantification, algorithm selection — with MATLAB examples. Validates: Layer 2 of the simulator and the model architecture choices behind the AviOS prognostic engine, including how to report RUL with confidence bounds (which the validation protocol in the Real-Data Ask spec requires).

3.3 International Journal of PHM + PHM Society Proceedings OPEN ACCESS

The field's flagship journal (IJPHM, ISSN 2153-2648) and the annual PHM Society / PHM Europe conference proceedings — all free. Directly relevant recent work includes PHM-driven spare-part inventory frameworks and system-dynamics analyses of PHM benefits to aircraft maintenance. Validates: keeps the failure models state-of-the-art, and the aerospace special issues are where your future validation paper belongs — publishing there is itself a credibility asset.

3.4 NASA Prognostics Center of Excellence Data Repository OPEN DATA

C-MAPSS (the classic 21-sensor turbofan RUL benchmark, 4 sub-datasets) and N-CMAPSS (2021, with real flight conditions — far more realistic degradation trajectories); mirrored at the PHM Society repository. Validates: Layer 1 synthetic telemetry realism and gives the prognostic engine a public benchmark — "our RUL model scores X on N-CMAPSS" is the one accuracy claim you can legitimately publish before operator data arrives, because anyone can reproduce it.

3.5 Moubray, RCM II + SAE JA1011/JA1012 BOOK + STANDARDS

Moubray's RCM II (Industrial Press) turned Nowlan & Heap into practice across industries; SAE JA1011/1012 define what may legitimately be called an RCM process. Validates: the failure-mode taxonomy and the scheduled-vs-condition-based decision logic in the simulator's Layer 3, in the vocabulary reliability engineers at your customers actually use.

5. Ongoing Media — the Standing Watch

  • Aviation Week — Inside MRO magazine + the MRO Podcast: the industry's trade press of record; where operator pain points, MRO capacity moves, and competitor announcements surface first.
  • MRO Americas / MRO Europe / MRO Asia-Pacific (Aviation Week events): where every buyer, competitor, and MRO software incumbent exhibits — the annual reality check on positioning.
  • IATA MCTG (Maintenance Cost Technical Group): guidelines and webinars; joining the MCX exchange later gives benchmarking depth competitors won't have.
  • ARSA annual market assessment and regulatory tracking; Skybrary as the free encyclopedic reference for any operational concept.
  • PHM Society annual conference + European conference: the technical frontier; the data challenges are recruiting grounds and benchmark opportunities.
