1. Thesis — the competition has moved from the hangar to the operating system

World-class MRO today is defined by physical plant and capability breadth. Aviation Technical Services (Everett, WA — the Seattle benchmark) runs 1.2M+ ft² of airframe, component, modification and paint capability and was Aviation Week's 2016 Independent MRO of the Year. Saudia Technic's new PIF-backed MRO Village in Jeddah is a ~1M m² (10.7M ft²) build with up to 11 hangars, four widebody bays simultaneously, and a Jet Propulsion Center test cell rated to 150,000 lbf. These are the right benchmarks for scale and capability.

But both — and every incumbent — still run on transactional systems-of-record (AMOS, TRAX, OASES) surrounded by spreadsheets, email, and tribal knowledge. The four numbers that decide MRO profitability — turnaround time, hangar yield, AOG hours, and inventory turns — are all governed by decisions made against a stale model of reality. DESIGN The next-generation MRO does not win with more square footage; it wins with a superior operating system: a live operational ontology in which every tail, part, technician, bay, and defect is an object with its decisions attached, and where AviOS prediction turns unscheduled events into scheduled ones. The physical plant is table stakes; the ontology is the moat.

3. The Palantir framework, applied to MRO

Palantir's operating model centres on the Ontology: a layer that connects integrated data to its real-world counterparts and pairs semantic elements (objects, properties, links) with kinetic elements (actions, functions, security) to "close the loop between AI/ML and operations." We adopt that framework wholesale — with one deliberate exception. Palantir markets its ontology as "a digital twin of an organization." AeroClip/AviOS does not use "digital twin" (it over-promises physical fidelity we don't claim); we say operational ontology. The distinction is the same one drawn in the AviOS Simulator spec.

3.1 Semantic layer — the objects (mapped to the AviOS entity model)

The ontology's core objects are the AviOS canonical entities (Simulator spec §6), which keeps the strategic model and the product one-to-one:

  • Aircraft (tail) — type/series, cumulative hours/cycles, status.
  • Component (serial, LLP) — installed serial, ATA chapter, TSN/CSN/TSO, life-limit remaining, and the AviOS-populated health index and RUL properties.
  • WorkOrder / MaintenanceEvent — MSG-3 task, Failure Effect Category (5 evident-safety … 9 hidden-non-safety), start/end, parts consumed.
  • Defect — PIREP/MAREP source, ATA chapter, MEL rectification category (A per-interval, B 3 days, C 10 days, D 120 days — calendar-based), resolving event.
  • Resource objects — HangarBay, TechnicianShift (with certifications), Part (rotable/consumable, BOM, supplier lead-time) — the finite resources the Simulator's Layer 3 DES already models.
  • Constraint objects — AirworthinessDirective (mandatory, non-deferrable deadline), regulatory rule (Part-145 / Part-CAMO / Part 43).

Links encode the physics: installed-on, deferred-under-MEL, requires-part, assigned-to-bay, performed-by-tech, superseded-by-AD, escalates-to-check. A rotable's serial moves between tails over its life — the link history is its provenance.

3.2 Kinetic layer — the decisions (where value is actually created)

Objects are nouns; decisions are verbs. Each action is permissioned, logged, and reversible — and one is hard-walled: DESIGN release-to-service is a CAMO / Part-145 human decision only. AviOS recommends and predicts; it never certifies, releases, or defers a defect on its own authority. That boundary is what keeps the system sellable to a regulator.

3.3 Where AviOS plugs in — the blueprint reuses the specs you already have

The three canonical AviOS documents are not separate from this blueprint — they are its build spec:
  • The Simulator pre-trains and hardens the ontology's prediction functions before any real data exists (de-risking cold-start).
  • The prognostic engine populates the Component object's health/RUL properties that the kinetic layer acts on.
  • The Minimal Real-Data Ask extract (five tables) is the ontology's initial data-integration payload — its tables map straight onto Aircraft, Component, MaintenanceEvent, Defect. The temporal-holdout validation protocol is how the prediction functions earn PREDICTIVE status.

5. Value creation — the four P&L levers

Ontology-native operations move each of the four numbers that decide MRO profitability. The mechanism is DESIGN-valid; the cost baselines are MARKET-valid (IATA MCX); the improvement magnitude is PREDICTIVE and is not asserted here as a number until pilot data proves it.

AOG cost is the operator's input, never our assertion. Quoted day-rates range from ~$10k (narrowbody, minor) to $150k+ (widebody, revenue loss included) with no single authoritative figure — so in any ROI model AOG cost is a customer-supplied variable with cited ranges as defaults. MARKET The macro tailwind is real and documented: MRO spend surpassed $136B in 2025, fleet age hit 13.4 years, and supply-chain constraints added $3.1B of maintenance cost — an aging, parts-starved fleet is exactly the market where prediction and pre-positioning are worth the most (Oliver Wyman; IATA).

7. Moat & positioning

  • vs systems-of-record (AMOS / TRAX / OASES): they are transactional ledgers; the ontology is the decision layer above them. Integrate, don't replace — the Real-Data Ask extract tables double as the integration surface.
  • vs Palantir Foundry itself: Foundry is horizontal. This is a vertical MRO ontology with aviation prediction and Part-145/CAMO guardrails baked in — the domain knowledge (MSG-3, ATA, MEL, RCM) is the moat, and it already lives in the AviOS skills.
  • vs OEM prognostics (GE, Rolls-Royce): an OEM sees only its own engines. The MRO ontology sees the whole tail across every system, OEM, and supplier — the cross-ATA, cross-vendor view no single OEM can hold.
  • The flywheel: more fleets → better base-rate calibration → better prediction → more fleets. DESIGN

8. Honesty boundary (the same discipline as every AviOS document)

  • Have now DESIGN MARKET: an architecture that matches how MRO actually works, and a documented, growing market with a real tailwind.
  • Do not have until pilot PREDICTIVE: observed precision on an operator's real history, and therefore any specific % improvement in TAT, AOG, or inventory. The Real-Data Ask protocol is the only path to it.
  • The physical MRO is real capital and real regulation. The software advantage rides on top of a certified Part-145 organization with a capability list and approved data — it does not bypass certification, and the buildout (hangars, backshops, engine test) is a genuine multi-year capital program, as Jeddah's own scale shows.
  • Licensing: MSG-3, iSpec 2200, and Spec 2000 are licensed A4A documents — the ontology encodes their structure and logic, never their text; licenses are purchased before any of it ships.
